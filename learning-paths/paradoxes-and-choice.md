# Paradoxes, Probability, and Rational Choice

Not every surprising conclusion is a contradiction. First specify the sample space, available information, and decision rule; then decide what remains philosophically unsettled.

## Learning objectives

- Identify inconsistent premises in a formal paradox.
- Compute a simple conditional probability.
- Distinguish prediction from causation and risk from ambiguity.

## Recommended sequence

1. [Sorites: When Does a Heap Begin?](../experiments/logic/sorites.md) — Start with a vague predicate.
2. [The Liar Paradox](../experiments/logic/liar.md) — Contrast vagueness with self-reference.
3. [Russell’s Paradox](../experiments/logic/russells-paradox.md) — Examine unrestricted set formation.
4. [The Monty Hall Problem](../experiments/mathematics/monty-hall.md) — Track the host’s conditional behavior.
5. [Bertrand’s Random Chord Paradox](../experiments/mathematics/bertrands-paradox.md) — Compare different randomization procedures.
6. [Sleeping Beauty](../experiments/mathematics/sleeping-beauty.md) — Identify the disputed updating principle.
7. [The St. Petersburg Paradox](../experiments/mathematics/st-petersburg-paradox.md) — Separate expected money from utility.
8. [Newcomb’s Problem](../experiments/time-and-causality/newcomb.md) — Contrast causal and evidential recommendations.
9. [The Ellsberg Paradox](../experiments/economics/ellsberg-paradox.md) — Compare known risks with ambiguous probabilities.

Read each article’s scenario first, write an initial answer, and then read the interpretations and sources. Spend as long as useful; there is no required schedule.

## Reflection questions

- Which disputes disappear once assumptions are explicit?
- When should a seemingly irrational preference lead us to revise a normative model?

## Final synthesis activity

Create a two-part worksheet: a fully specified probability puzzle with a calculation, and a decision problem with competing rules. Explain why the first has a determinate answer under its premises while the second may retain a substantive dispute.
