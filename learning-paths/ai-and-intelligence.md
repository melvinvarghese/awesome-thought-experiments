# Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Intelligence

Approach machine intelligence through behavior, understanding, goals, and control. These are distinct questions, and success on one does not automatically answer the others.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish behavioral tests from theories of consciousness.
- Explain how a specified goal can diverge from human intent.
- Evaluate what a containment scenario does and does not demonstrate.

## Recommended sequence

1. [The Turing Test](../experiments/artificial-intelligence/turing-test.md) — Ask what evidence a behavioral comparison supplies.
2. [Chinese Room](../experiments/consciousness/chinese-room.md) — Compare competent output with semantic understanding.
3. [China Brain](../experiments/consciousness/china-brain.md) — Question intuitions about substrate and scale.
4. [The Paperclip Maximizer](../experiments/artificial-intelligence/paperclip-maximizer.md) — Identify the assumptions behind instrumental goal pursuit.
5. [The AI Box Scenario](../experiments/artificial-intelligence/ai-box.md) — Separate a persuasion demonstration from a security guarantee.
6. [Experience Machine](../experiments/ethics/experience-machine.md) — Ask what welfare an optimized world should preserve.
7. [The Veil of Ignorance and Original Position](../experiments/society-and-justice/veil-of-ignorance.md) — Consider how uncertainty about your position changes design choices.

Read each article’s scenario first, write an initial answer, and then read the interpretations and sources. Spend as long as useful; there is no required schedule.

## Reflection questions

- What evidence would change your judgment that a system understands?
- Which premises would need empirical support before a vivid AI scenario could support a forecast?

## Final synthesis activity

Write an evaluation brief for a hypothetical assistant. Give separate tests for capability, reliability, human control, and welfare assumptions. State which tests are empirical and which conclusions remain philosophical. Avoid claiming that a thought experiment predicts a deployment outcome.
