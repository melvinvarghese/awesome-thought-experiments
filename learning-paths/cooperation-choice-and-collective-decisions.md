# Cooperation, Choice, and Collective Decisions

This path covers the problems that arise when individual choices combine: when cooperation is risky, when majorities cycle, and when tiny probabilities dominate a calculation. The models are simplified, so ask what real institutions add.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish a cooperation problem driven by temptation from one driven by risk.
- Explain why aggregating preferences is harder than it looks.
- Evaluate when expected-value reasoning fails or is exploited.

## Recommended sequence

1. [The Prisoner’s Dilemma](../experiments/economics/prisoners-dilemma.md) — Begin with the model of mutual defection.
2. [The Stag Hunt](../experiments/economics/stag-hunt.md) — Contrast it with a case where trust is the problem.
3. [The Tragedy of the Commons](../experiments/economics/tragedy-of-the-commons.md) — Ask how shared resources are overused.
4. [The Condorcet Paradox](../experiments/society-and-justice/condorcet-paradox.md) — See how majority preferences can cycle.
5. [Arrow’s Impossibility Theorem](../experiments/society-and-justice/arrows-impossibility-theorem.md) — Learn why no ranking rule satisfies every fairness condition.
6. [Sen’s Paradox of the Paretian Liberal](../experiments/society-and-justice/sens-liberal-paradox.md) — Ask whether a right to liberty conflicts with efficiency.
7. [The Veil of Ignorance and Original Position](../experiments/society-and-justice/veil-of-ignorance.md) — Consider choosing rules without knowing your place.
8. [Pascal’s Wager](../experiments/economics/pascals-wager.md) — Turn to decisions with infinite stakes.
9. [Pascal’s Mugging](../experiments/economics/pascals-mugging.md) — Ask how to resist manipulation by tiny probabilities.

Read each article’s scenario first, write an initial answer, and then read the interpretations and sources. Spend as long as useful; there is no required schedule.

## Reflection questions

- Which institutions turn a stag hunt into a stable cooperative outcome?
- Is a cycle in majority preferences a problem for the group or for the procedure?

## Final synthesis activity

Design a rule for a small club to choose a meeting time or a budget. Say which of the problems in this path it avoids, which it faces, and which condition you chose to give up. Label the rule as your own proposal.
