# The Universe, Time, and Physical Reality

Track the difference between an imagined test, a mathematical model, and an empirical result. A vivid picture alone does not establish a physical law.

## Learning objectives

- Explain the role of idealization in physical reasoning.
- Distinguish relativity effects from logical contradiction.
- Separate quantum predictions from interpretations.

## Recommended sequence

1. [Galileo’s Tied Falling Bodies](../experiments/physics/galileos-falling-bodies.md) — Identify the assumption tested by tying falling bodies together.
2. [Einstein Chases a Light Beam](../experiments/physics/einsteins-light-beam.md) — Follow the tension between chasing light and electrodynamics.
3. [Einstein’s Elevator](../experiments/physics/einsteins-elevator.md) — Explore the local scope of the equivalence principle.
4. [The Twin Paradox](../experiments/physics/twin-paradox.md) — Compare elapsed proper times rather than symmetrical slogans.
5. [Maxwell’s Demon](../experiments/physics/maxwells-demon.md) — Include the demon and its information processing in the accounting.
6. [Schrödinger’s Cat](../experiments/physics/schrodingers-cat.md) — Track the measurement problem without treating a cat as a laboratory recipe.
7. [The Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen Argument](../experiments/physics/epr.md) — Separate locality, completeness, and prediction.
8. [Wigner’s Friend](../experiments/physics/wigners-friend.md) — Compare the viewpoints of an observer and an outside observer.
9. [The Grandfather Paradox](../experiments/time-and-causality/grandfather-paradox.md) — Distinguish a causal loop from an inconsistent history.

Read each article’s scenario first, write an initial answer, and then read the interpretations and sources. Spend as long as useful; there is no required schedule.

## Reflection questions

- Where does an idealization stop being harmless?
- Which empirical evidence constrains an interpretation without uniquely proving it?

## Final synthesis activity

Choose one classical and one quantum example. For each write: original aim, simplifying assumptions, theoretical claim, relevant later evidence, and remaining interpretive question. Do not call a philosophical interpretation an experimental discovery.
