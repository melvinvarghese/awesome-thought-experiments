## Checks performed

- Dataset parsed and validated against the supplied JSON Schema using the standard-library evaluator in `scripts/validate.py`. That evaluator supports the schema keywords used here and fails closed on unsupported validation keywords; it is not a general-purpose JSON Schema implementation.
- Validator checked required fields, duplicate IDs/slugs/paths, category and difficulty values, article existence, metadata agreement, all ten nonempty article sections, reference presence and URL syntax, related IDs and their article links, README/dataset consistency, and relative file and heading links throughout Markdown.
- Eighteen mutation/baseline tests checked that the validator accepts the collection and rejects representative missing metadata, duplicate IDs/slugs, invalid categories/difficulties, missing files/references, malformed URLs/JSON, invalid related IDs, README omissions, missing or empty sections, mismatched article metadata, broken file/heading links, and unlisted articles.
- GitHub workflow and three issue forms parsed as YAML and their expected structure was asserted. The workflow’s two Python commands ran locally. No GitHub-hosted workflow was executed during production; check the repository’s Actions tab for current results.
- On 31 October 2026 the validator and its tests were changed to read and write files as UTF-8. Before that change they failed on Windows, where Python’s default encoding cannot decode the typographic characters used in the articles. After the 57 new entries were added, the validator passed on 132 entries and all eighteen tests passed.
- For the expansion, every new reference was requested by script and its HTTP status, page title and topic-keyword counts were recorded in [research/expansion.md](research/expansion.md). This confirms that the pages exist and discuss the topic; it is not a reading of them.
- SVG parsed as XML, rendered with Inkscape, and visually inspected; a title-size adjustment improved spacing. No third-party artwork is bundled.
- Scanned for empty files, unfinished-text markers, and Unicode replacement characters. Reviewed article samples, the learning paths, connections, and navigation for clarity and distinctions. This was an editorial review with AI assistance, not an independent human peer review or a formal spelling certification.
- Final ZIP opened successfully, passed CRC checks, and was compared against the repository file inventory and bytes. The extracted copy passed the validator and all eighteen tests.

The audit ledger remains outside the public data schema; no public status badge or expert-review claim was added. Many original historical books and articles were accessed through scholarly encyclopedia discussions or teaching materials rather than inspected in full; the batch ledgers give exact passage locations and limits. The Utility Monster audit read Nozick’s chapter 3, page 41, in a university-hosted PDF reproduction, while Chappell’s related paper remained abstract/record-only. The AI-box audit consulted Yudkowsky’s retrospective account and contemporary 2002 mailing-list messages, but not private transcripts. The Spandrels audit read relevant passages in a reproduction of the full 1979 paper, Gould’s 1997 clarification, and Gould and Vrba’s 1982 exaptation paper; the third-party reproduction and OCR may contain errors. Wigner’s 1961/1962 edition dating remains uncertain. The Four-Case audit did not directly access Pereboom’s 2001 chapter; the Kripke audit used a selected excerpt rather than the complete book. The completed philosophy audit records stronger direct access obtained for Singer, Nagel, and Leibniz materials. In batch 10, Quine’s 1970 passages were read in a third-party course-hosted scan; the DOI landing page at the Philosophy Documentation Center provides metadata but gates full text. Einstein’s train exposition was consulted in Lawson’s 1920 English translation; the 1916 German first-edition title page was not checked. The Nietzsche notebook fragment cited for a cosmological reading was not directly accessed, so Eternal Recurrence remains partially source-checked and relies on the cited secondary account for that fragment. Batch 11 read Galileo’s relevant ship and tower passages in the full Drake translation, but did not collate the original Italian; Heraclitus’s book is lost, so this audit used later ancient witnesses and reports the Graham–Taran disagreement rather than resolving it. Thomson’s and Benacerraf’s relevant passages were read in hosted copies; the complete papers were not read systematically. Batch 12 added Philo’s later testimony and Bowin’s discussion for Dion and Theon, but did not access Chrysippus’s lost original; the Grelling–Nelson paper was represented by a translated passage in the SEP, not read in a primary copy, and the exact 1907/1908 publication chronology remains unsettled; McTaggart’s relevant pages were read in a full-text scan reproduction, while the publisher’s full text remained gated.

The new batches 13–17 likewise retain access and interpretation limits. Zeno’s original work is lost and the Arrow/Dichotomy wording is reconstructed from later testimony. The Cave article’s approximate c. 375 BCE date appears in an automatically generated Project Gutenberg catalogue summary and is not confirmed by the consulted scholarly chronology sources, so that record remains partial. The Idle Argument relies on later ancient witnesses; Galileo’s Paradox was read in an English translation rather than the Italian original; Moore’s relevant passages were checked in a 1993 reprint rather than the 1939 issue. The Statue and Clay and Problem of the Many entries map competing metaphysical accounts rather than resolving them; Condorcet’s voting-cycle claims remain conditional on the stated preference profile. The formal epistemology entries do not establish a consensus response. For the final batch, Mill’s Other Minds passage and Dretske’s original mule article were accessed through secondary quotations/discussion rather than the primary full text; Lewis’s mad-pain cases are stipulated, and an objection remains unresolved; the Stag Hunt payoff matrix is illustrative and the original Rousseau text was read in English translation. No named human subject-matter expert reviewed batches 13–17.

No automated live-link sweep was run: retrieval establishes only the access levels documented during production, and the validator checks URL syntax rather than current availability. No claim is made that every historical priority or controversial interpretation was independently settled. Unresolved philosophical questions remain unresolved. AI-box roleplay, evolutionary analogies, formal paradoxes, and games are not represented as established empirical demonstrations.
