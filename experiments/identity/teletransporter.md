# Teletransporter

**Category:** identity  
**Associated thinker(s):** Derek Parfit  
**Original publication or approximate period:** Reasons and Persons (1984), modern influential presentation  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Themes:** survival, copying, continuity  
**Related experiments:** fission, ship-of-theseus  
**Kind:** thought experiment  
**Research checked:** 2026-10-30

## 1. The Scenario

A scanner records your body and brain in exhaustive detail, destroys the original, and constructs an exact counterpart elsewhere. The arrival remembers entering the scanner and continues your projects. Now change one condition: the scanner fails to destroy you, while producing the arrival as usual. The person who stayed behind meets the person who arrived. Both have the same apparent claim to your remembered past.

## 2. The Central Question

Does perfect psychological reconstruction preserve you, or merely produce someone much like you?

## 3. Why It Matters

The contrast between ordinary transport and duplication tests whether a pattern of information is sufficient for personal identity. It also separates the question of strict identity from questions about what someone should care about in a future successor.

The duplicate version is a useful stress test because the arrival’s internal state need not change when the original remains alive. If your verdict about the arrival changes solely because another person exists elsewhere, explain why identity is sensitive to that fact. If it does not change, explain how one predecessor could be identical to two distinct later people. Either answer exposes a commitment that the simpler destruction-and-reconstruction story can conceal. You may also conclude that the practical value of continuation is clearer than the question of strict numerical identity.

## 4. Major Interpretations

Psychological-continuity approaches may regard the arrival as a continuer, while adding nonbranching conditions when copies compete. Animalist approaches locate persistence in the continued life of the human organism. Parfit’s reductionist treatment suggests that psychological relations can matter even where strict identity fails to track what we ordinarily value.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

The case does not show that actual teleportation is technically possible. Quantum teleportation transfers quantum states under specified conditions and is not this imagined transport of a person. A duplicate’s sincere recollection is evidence of psychological resemblance, not automatically proof of numerical identity.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. Would you pay to enter the scanner if destruction occurred before reconstruction?
2. Why should the survival of an extra copy affect the first arrival’s identity?
3. Could you care about a successor even if that successor were not you?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

The branch-line variant preserves the original and thereby forces rival claims into view. Gradual replacement and partial reconstruction cases test continuity differently; they are recognized neighboring debates, not solutions by definition.
