# China Brain

**Category:** consciousness  
**Associated thinker(s):** Ned Block  
**Original publication or approximate period:** Troubles with Functionalism (1978; later reprint versions)  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**Themes:** functionalism, systems, qualia  
**Related experiments:** chinese-room, fading-and-dancing-qualia  
**Kind:** thought experiment  
**Research checked:** 2026-10-30

## 1. The Scenario

Imagine a very large population coordinating by radios according to instructions that reproduce the causal organization of a human brain. Messages between participants play the role of signals between neurons, and an artificial body supplies inputs and outputs. The relevant organization is assumed to be sufficiently detailed; a vague resemblance to a network is not enough. Would the whole arrangement have experiences?

## 2. The Central Question

Can the right functional organization produce consciousness in an extremely unfamiliar physical implementation?

## 3. Why It Matters

Block uses the case to put pressure on functionalism: a system might satisfy a proposed functional description while seeming to lack experience. The scenario also exposes a risk of judging a whole system by the mental capacities of its individual components.

Draw a boundary around the alleged conscious system. It includes a pattern of communication, coordinated states, and connections to inputs and outputs. If you focus only on one participant, you may be evaluating the wrong candidate, just as one neuron is not ordinarily proposed as the complete human mind. On the other hand, placing a boundary around a collection does not make it conscious. The defender still needs to say why this organization is the relevant one, and the critic needs to identify what it lacks beyond an unfamiliar appearance.

## 4. Major Interpretations

An absent-qualia response says the organized population still lacks the subjective character of a person. Functionalists may accept consciousness at the system level, arguing that unfamiliar size and speed do not establish its absence. Others question whether the stipulated implementation really captures every causally relevant detail.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

The example is not an empirical claim about the population of China or a theory of national character. The country supplies an imagined number of participants. Participants’ individual consciousness is separate from the disputed consciousness of the larger coordinated system.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. Why should biological neurons be acceptable components but communicating people seem absurd?
2. Would slowing a system change whether it feels anything?
3. What evidence could distinguish an intuition about scale from an argument about consciousness?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

Absent-qualia cases vary the implementing substrate. Chalmers’s gradual replacement argument approaches a related issue through continuity, asking what would happen if neurons were replaced one at a time while preserving organization.
