# Blockhead

**Category:** artificial-intelligence  
**Type:** thought experiment  
**Associated thinker(s):** Ned Block  
**Original publication or approximate period:** Block, “Psychologism and Behaviorism” (Philosophical Review, 1981)  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Themes:** behaviourism, Turing test, intelligence  
**Related experiments:** [The Turing Test](turing-test.md); [Chinese Room](../consciousness/chinese-room.md); [China Brain](../consciousness/china-brain.md)

## 1. The Scenario

Imagine a machine built to pass a conversation test for one hour. Its designers have written out, in advance, every sensible exchange that could occur in an hour of typed conversation, and stored the possible sentences and the correct replies in a gigantic lookup table. When the interrogator types something, the machine finds the matching entry and prints the stored reply.

The machine behaves like an intelligent conversation partner, but it does no reasoning and no problem-solving. All it does is look things up.

## 2. The Central Question

If a system’s behaviour is indistinguishable from an intelligent being’s, is that enough to say it is intelligent?

## 3. Why It Matters

Block argues that behaviour alone is not what makes a system intelligent: the way the behaviour is produced matters. This targets the idea that the Turing test, or any purely behavioural criterion, is a sufficient test of intelligence.

The case does not say passing the test is irrelevant. It says the inference from behaviour to intelligence depends on assumptions about how the behaviour was produced.

## 4. Major Interpretations

**Block’s own view** is that the example refutes behaviourism and supports a psychologism requiring some internal information-processing. **Defenders of the test** say that Blockhead is physically impossible and that the test is evidence, not definition. **Functionalists** might say that the right internal organization is what counts. **Skeptics** note that intuitions about lookup tables are unreliable.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

Blockhead is not a proposal for building a chatbot. The table would be astronomically large, far beyond the number of atoms in the universe, so the point is conceptual, not practical. It also does not show that machines cannot think, only that behaviour alone does not settle the question.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. What would you need to know about a machine to decide whether it understands?
2. Does the impossibility of building Blockhead weaken the argument?
3. Would you say a human who memorized a script is intelligent in the conversation?
4. How does Blockhead differ from the Chinese Room?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

Versions shorten the time or restrict the topics so that the table is smaller. The idea can also be applied to other behaviours, such as playing chess by lookup.
