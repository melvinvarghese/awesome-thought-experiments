# Wigner’s Friend

**Category:** physics  
**Associated thinker(s):** Eugene Wigner  
**Original publication or approximate period:** “Remarks on the Mind–Body Problem,” early 1960s (commonly cited 1961; edition dates vary)  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**Type:** thought experiment  
**Themes:** observers; measurement; consistency  
**Related experiments:** schrodingers-cat; epr

## 1. The Scenario

A friend inside an isolated laboratory measures a quantum system and records a definite result. Outside, Wigner treats the system, apparatus, and friend as a single physical system evolving unitarily. His description can include a superposition of differently correlated records.

The friend’s report and Wigner’s quantum description have to be related without quietly changing the theory between viewpoints. In the idealized scenario, Wigner would need extraordinary control to test interference involving the entire laboratory. A normal laboratory with information leaking into its environment does not meet those assumptions.

## 2. The Central Question

Can observers consistently apply quantum theory to other observers while each experiences a definite outcome?

## 3. Why It Matters

The scenario places the boundary between observer and observed under pressure. It also shows that phrases such as “a measurement happened” may conceal different physical commitments. One must specify whether collapse is a real event, an update of knowledge, or unnecessary to the complete theory.

## 4. Major Interpretations

**Wigner’s historical response** connected conscious observation with physical collapse. **Everettian and relational responses** reject that specific consciousness-based conclusion while offering different accounts of observer-relative records. **Objective collapse theories** revise unitary evolution under specified conditions. These alternatives do not all satisfy the same assumptions, and the scenario alone does not decide between them.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

The original argument does not establish that consciousness creates reality. Extended-friend no-go theorems show the inconsistency of particular assumption sets; they do not show that every quantum theory is self-contradictory. Experimental analogues using small systems are not straightforward experiments on a coherent human mind.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. Is a friend’s record an additional physical fact, an observer-relative fact, or an incomplete description?
2. Which interactions would have to be controlled to reverse the whole laboratory measurement?
3. When comparing two observers, are you using the same meaning of “outcome” throughout?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

Extended Wigner’s-friend scenarios use several observers to derive no-go results under explicit assumptions. Read those assumptions before interpreting headlines about “contradictory realities.”
