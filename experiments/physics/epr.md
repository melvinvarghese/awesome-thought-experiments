# The Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen Argument

**Category:** physics  
**Associated thinker(s):** Albert Einstein; Boris Podolsky; Nathan Rosen  
**Original publication or approximate period:** “Can Quantum-Mechanical Description of Physical Reality Be Considered Complete?” (1935)  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**Type:** thought experiment  
**Themes:** entanglement; locality; completeness  
**Related experiments:** schrodingers-cat; wigners-friend

## 1. The Scenario

Prepare two systems with strongly correlated positions and momenta, then separate them. In the idealized original argument, a position measurement on one lets you predict the other’s position; choosing momentum instead lets you predict its momentum. EPR propose that a quantity predictable with certainty without disturbing the distant system corresponds to an element of physical reality.

If the distant system is unaffected by the local measurement choice, the argument seeks simultaneous elements of reality for both quantities, although the ordinary wave function does not assign them both sharp values. The alternatives are counterfactual measurement choices, not a claim that one run measures both quantities sharply.

## 2. The Central Question

Is quantum mechanics complete if local measurement choices allow certain predictions about distant systems?

## 3. Why It Matters

EPR clarified a tension between a picture of separately existing distant systems and the structure of quantum states. Its legacy is not just a debate about interpretation: later Bell arguments yield constraints on broad classes of locally causal accounts that can be confronted with experiments. The original 1935 reasoning and those later experimental tests must still be distinguished.

## 4. Major Interpretations

**EPR’s proposed conclusion** is that the wave function is incomplete given their reality and locality reasoning. **Bohr’s response** challenges how the conditions for predictions and measurement contexts enter that inference. **Later Bell analysis** shows that a large class of local hidden-variable explanations cannot reproduce quantum correlations. Modern experimental violations constrain that class, rather than proving that controllable signals can travel faster than light.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

Entanglement does not let an observer select a distant measurement outcome. The original scenario uses position and momentum; the familiar pair of spins is a later Bohm-style version. “Realism has been disproved” is an overbroad description of results that depend on precise assumptions.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. Which premise turns a prediction into an element of reality?
2. Why does choosing what to measure locally matter to the completeness argument?
3. How does a statistical Bell test differ from an ideal certainty-based EPR argument?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

Bohm’s spin formulation makes the structure easier to describe with discrete outcomes. Bell inequalities replace a purely philosophical exchange with conditional mathematical bounds and empirical tests.
