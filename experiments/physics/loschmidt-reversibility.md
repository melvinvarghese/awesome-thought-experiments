# Loschmidt’s Reversibility Objection

**Category:** physics  
**Type:** thought experiment  
**Associated thinker(s):** Josef Loschmidt, Ludwig Boltzmann  
**Original publication or approximate period:** Loschmidt’s objection (1870s) and Boltzmann’s replies  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**Themes:** entropy, time asymmetry, statistical mechanics  
**Related experiments:** [Maxwell’s Demon](maxwells-demon.md); [Laplace’s Demon](../time-and-causality/laplaces-demon.md)

## 1. The Scenario

A gas in a box spreads out from a corner until it fills the box evenly, and its entropy increases. Now suppose that, at some instant, the velocity of every molecule is exactly reversed. The laws that govern collisions are the same in both time directions, so the molecules will retrace their paths: the gas will gather back into the corner, and its entropy will decrease.

If such reversed states are possible, then the law that entropy increases cannot follow from the dynamics of the molecules alone.

## 2. The Central Question

If the underlying laws do not distinguish past from future, why does entropy increase in one direction of time?

## 3. Why It Matters

The objection challenges the attempt to derive the second law of thermodynamics from mechanics. Boltzmann’s reply was that entropy increase is overwhelmingly probable, not strictly necessary, because far more microstates look like equilibrium than not. That shifts the question to why the early universe had such low entropy.

The result connects the arrow of time to cosmology and to the choice of initial conditions.

## 4. Major Interpretations

**Boltzmann’s statistical view** says that reversed states exist but are extremely rare. **Initial-condition views** say the time asymmetry arises from the low-entropy past. **Dynamical approaches** try to add asymmetric laws. **Critical views** question whether the probabilities used are justified.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

The objection does not show that entropy decreases in practice. Reversing velocities of every molecule is practically impossible. It also does not refute statistical mechanics; it shows the second law is statistical. It is distinct from Maxwell’s demon, though both concern limits on the second law.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. Why do we never see a broken egg reassemble?
2. Does a statistical law count as a law?
3. What explains the low entropy of the early universe?
4. Could there be regions of the universe where time seems reversed?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

The Poincaré recurrence theorem, raised by Zermelo, is a related objection. It is a separate argument with a different premise.
