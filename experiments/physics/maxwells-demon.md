# Maxwell’s Demon

**Category:** physics  
**Associated thinker(s):** James Clerk Maxwell  
**Original publication or approximate period:** Private proposal (1867); Theory of Heat (1871)  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Type:** thought experiment  
**Themes:** entropy; information; thermodynamics  
**Related experiments:** schrodingers-cat; st-petersburg-paradox

## 1. The Scenario

A gas fills two compartments joined by a small doorway. A hypothetical controller can distinguish faster and slower molecules. It admits selected fast molecules to one side and selected slow molecules to the other, apparently creating a temperature difference without the usual expenditure of work.

The gas alone appears to move away from equilibrium. But the controller, gate, memory, power supply, and eventual resetting process also belong in the physical account. A useful analysis must identify a full cycle rather than inspect only the successful sorting stage.

## 2. The Central Question

Can microscopic information and selective control be used to extract work in a repeatable cycle without an offsetting thermodynamic cost?

## 3. Why It Matters

The demon asks where the boundary of a thermodynamic system should be drawn. It connects physical information processing with the difference between a temporarily ordered subsystem and a complete engine. This makes it a productive test of explanations of entropy, even when no literal tiny observer is imagined.

## 4. Major Interpretations

**Information-thermodynamic accounts** examine measurement, feedback, and especially the resetting of memory; Landauer-style arguments locate a cost in logically irreversible erasure under relevant conditions. **Foundational critiques** ask whether these arguments assume the thermodynamic principles they are supposed to establish. The debate concerns explanatory details and scope, not an accepted construction of an unlimited perpetual-motion machine.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

“Information is energy” is too crude: a bit does not possess a universal energy independent of its implementation and environment. Measurement need not always dissipate the same fixed amount. A local entropy decrease is not automatically a violation of a law about a larger closed system.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. Which parts of the apparatus must return to their initial state for an engine cycle to repeat?
2. What changes if the controller has an initially blank but finite memory?
3. How would you distinguish using stored free energy from violating a thermodynamic constraint?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

Szilard’s one-molecule engine isolates the role of measurement and feedback. Modern feedback experiments implement limited relatives of the scenario, with explicit accounting of resources rather than a supernatural being.
