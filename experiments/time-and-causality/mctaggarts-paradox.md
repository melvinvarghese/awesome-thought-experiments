# McTaggart’s Paradox of Time

**Category:** time-and-causality  
**Type:** argument  
**Associated thinker(s):** J. M. E. McTaggart  
**Original publication or approximate period:** McTaggart, “The Unreality of Time” (Mind, 1908)  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**Themes:** A-series and B-series, change, tense  
**Related experiments:** [The Grandfather Paradox](grandfather-paradox.md); [The Twin Paradox](../physics/twin-paradox.md)

## 1. The Scenario

McTaggart distinguishes two ways of ordering events in time. In the A-series, events are ordered as past, present or future, and these properties change: an event that is future becomes present and then past. In the B-series, events are ordered by the relations earlier than and later than, which do not change.

He argues, first, that time requires change, and that change requires the A-series. Second, he argues that the A-series is contradictory, since every event has all three properties (past, present and future) even though they are incompatible. Hence, time is unreal.

## 2. The Central Question

Is there a coherent notion of time that includes real change, and if not, is time real?

## 3. Why It Matters

The argument divides contemporary philosophy of time. Some accept that the A-series is essential to time and look for a way to make it consistent. Others deny that time needs the A-series and defend a view in which tense is not fundamental.

It also connects with physics, where relativity seems to support a B-series picture, and with debates about whether the present is special.

## 4. Major Interpretations

**Tenseless (B-theory) views** reject the first step: time is real and consists of earlier and later relations, with tense a feature of perspective. **Tensed (A-theory) views** reject the second step, arguing that the contradiction depends on treating tenses as simultaneous properties. **Presentist and growing-block views** adjust what exists. **McTaggart’s own view** is that time is unreal.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

McTaggart’s claim is not that clocks fail, but that the concept of time as including change is incoherent. Also, his A-series and B-series are not rival measurements; they are different orderings. The terms are his and are used with technical meanings.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. Does real change require that events change from future to present to past?
2. Is the claim that an event is past, present and future really contradictory?
3. Can we make sense of change using only earlier and later?
4. What would be lost if the present were not special?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

Later philosophers developed versions of the argument that avoid McTaggart’s specific commitments. Its challenge is to explain what, if anything, distinguishes now from other times.
