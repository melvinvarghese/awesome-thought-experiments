# The Bootstrap / Causal-Loop Paradox

**Category:** time-and-causality  
**Type:** paradox  
**Associated thinker(s):** David Lewis (influential analysis)  
**Original publication or approximate period:** Longstanding time-travel tradition; modern philosophical analysis in Lewis (1976)  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Themes:** causation, explanation, time travel  
**Related experiments:** [The Grandfather Paradox](grandfather-paradox.md); [Russell’s Five-Minute World](../epistemology/russells-five-minute-world.md)

## 1. The Scenario

A researcher receives a notebook containing a theorem. Years later, after learning how to travel backward in time, the researcher delivers a copy of that notebook to their younger self. The younger self then becomes the researcher who makes the delivery. Every local transfer has a cause, yet no one in the story independently proves the theorem.

Assume the information is copied without alteration and that the overall history is consistent. This informational version avoids some physical complications of a single material notebook repeatedly aging around a loop. The challenge concerns origin and explanation before it concerns engineering.

## 2. The Central Question

Can a causal loop be coherent even when the information or event sequence has no independent starting point?

## 3. Why It Matters

The example separates two objections often conflated in popular accounts. Contradictory events cannot all occur in one consistent history. An unusual or incomplete explanation need not be a contradiction. To reject the loop, one must say what explanatory or physical requirement it violates.

## 4. Major Interpretations

**Consistency response:** if each event fits with the others, a loop need not be logically impossible. Causal explanation might be circular without generating both a proposition and its negation.

**Explanatory objection:** explaining each link by another link may fail to explain why the entire loop exists or contains this information. Whether every such fact must have a further explanation is a substantive philosophical commitment.

**Physical caution:** a coherent narrative does not show that the required spacetime, copying processes, or stability conditions can exist. Questions about entropy and material deterioration require a specific physical model.

## 5. Common Misunderstandings

“No original author” does not mean “no cause anywhere.” The local causes are stated in the story. Nor is every circular explanation a physical causal loop: people can cite one another without time travel. A loop containing inconsistent instructions must be analyzed separately from a consistent but originless theorem.

## 6. Think for Yourself

1. What remains unexplained after every transfer has been causally accounted for?
2. Could two equally consistent loops contain different theorem statements?
3. Would replacing the notebook with a spoken message remove all physical concerns?
4. Do you require an external explanation for every complete causal history, including non-looping ones?

## 7. Variations and Extensions

Objects, instructions, and information can circulate in different versions. A physical object must satisfy aging and maintenance constraints along its personal history. A self-consistent message need not create a grandfather-style contradiction, although a changed message can introduce one.
