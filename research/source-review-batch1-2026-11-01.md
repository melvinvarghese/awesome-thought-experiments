# Source review: first high-risk batch

## What changed

All three PDF-dependent entries received a direct source check and an independent read-only review. The largest correction was to the simulation argument: Bostrom’s first horn is a near-zero fraction of human-level technological civilizations reaching posthumanity, not “almost all civilizations go extinct.” The paper’s third horn is a possible consequence, not an assumption.

- **[The Simulation Argument](../experiments/epistemology/simulation-argument.md):** The article now separates the assumptions about consciousness and computing from the three-way conclusion. It also makes clear that Bostrom’s personal-credence step is conditional on the “bland indifference principle” and relevant evidence. Bostrom’s paper argues for a disjunction; his 2005 [reply to Weatherson](https://pdf.stafforini.com/bostrom-2005-simulation-argument-reply.pdf) says it does not establish that the simulation branch is more likely. The article notes the debate without claiming it is settled. Weatherson’s [PhilArchive abstract](https://philarchive.org/rec/WEAAYA) was consulted; the full paper was not accessible in this review.
- **[Pascal’s Mugging](../experiments/economics/pascals-mugging.md):** The entry now states the conditions Bostrom actually uses: unbounded utility, linear happy-day valuation, a finite promised reward, and no risk aversion or temporal discounting. It no longer presents the puzzle as a general result about every improbable claim. It credits Yudkowsky’s [2007 post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a5JAiTdytou3Jg749/pascal-s-mugging-tiny-probabilities-of-vast-utilities) with naming the problem while distinguishing his example from Bostrom’s. A later response by Hiller and Hasan is described as one proposal, not a consensus solution; their [peer-reviewed article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0012217323000392) proposes considering symmetric rewards available if Pascal declines.
- **[The Orthogonality Thesis](../experiments/artificial-intelligence/orthogonality-thesis.md):** The definition now uses Bostrom’s “capacity for instrumental reasoning,” preserves “more or less” and its qualifications, and separates final goals from instrumental subgoals. Two later arguments are presented as debate, not settled refutations: Müller and Cannon’s [critique](https://doi.org/10.1111/rati.12320) of a common inference to AI existential risk, and Sharadin’s [analysis](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-024-02212-9) of goal-promotion accounts used in instrumental-convergence arguments.
