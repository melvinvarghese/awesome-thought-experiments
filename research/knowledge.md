## Limitations and attribution decisions

- Many original books and papers were not read in full. For those entries, historical and argumentative claims were checked against the cited scholarly surveys, not against a complete primary-source collation.
- Russell’s five-minute hypothesis and Plato’s ring narrative were checked in online primary texts. Jowett’s Plato translation has historical interpretive choices.
- The 1814 Laplace edition was checked through ETH’s digitized catalog/OCR page; the conceptual account relies on the SEP determinism survey.
- The donkey is not found in Buridan’s writings; its name is preserved only with that warning.
- No definitive inventor is asserted for grandfather, bootstrap, or surprise-examination puzzles. Eubulides attribution is traditional.
- The original position and veil of ignorance share one article; barber is a Russell variation. Formal paradoxes, models, and arguments are labeled rather than misclassified as empirical experiments.
- Clinical vision restoration and modern physical theories are mentioned cautiously; no new empirical or technical consensus is inferred.
- External retrieval confirms access and source coverage at production time; it is not a guarantee of future URL availability.
